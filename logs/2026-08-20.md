- **Important new information from Carolina: the measurement interferometer's
  LO arm is deliberately length-matched to the DUT arm's round trip,
  assumed ~1 m (2x the assumed 50 cm one-way fiber).** This changes what
  the z-axis means for the measurement (Ch1/Ch3) signal: it is NOT the
  absolute one-way distance to the reflector, it is the *residual*
  between the true round-trip DUT path and the matched LO arm, converted
  through the same z=c*tau/(2*n_g) formula -- i.e. z_observed =
  |L_true_one_way - L_match_one_way| (absolute value, because a real-valued
  signal + a plain FFT cannot distinguish "reflector past the match point"
  from "reflector short of it" -- both fold onto the same positive z).
  With L_match assumed = 0.5 m, this reframes the 47.4 mm peak as a
  candidate for the ACTUAL fiber-end reflection: true one-way length
  ~= 0.5 m +/- 0.0474 m, i.e. **45.26 cm or 54.74 cm** (sign ambiguous).
  This also retroactively fits the "LO-match point" language already used
  for the OLD 46.4 mm peak in HANDOVER.md section 1 -- possibly that peak
  was always the fiber, not a separate unexplained artifact. The 1109.4 mm
  far peak does NOT fit this residual picture cleanly (would need
  L_true ~= 1.609 m, unsupported by anything else) -- still unexplained,
  most likely a separate feature.
  **Open, not resolved:** need a discriminating test (HANDOVER's own
  house rule -- "every hypothesis... killed by a measurement, not a
  better story") to (a) settle the sign ambiguity and (b) confirm whether
  47.4 mm really is the fiber: deliberately change the LO-arm match length
  by a small known amount and see whether/how the near peak shifts.

- **Question answered: is dropping the first 10 nm (saturated) costly?**
  Resolution with the full clean 50 nm span would have been ~16.3 um;
  with the 40 nm that's left, it's ~20.2 um (measured: 20.87 um, close
  enough given rounding/window-shape factors) -- about **24% worse
  spatial resolution**, not dramatic. The Nyquist **range** is unaffected
  (unchanged at ~8.15-8.18 m across every run, trimmed or not) since it
  depends on the per-sample frequency step, which dropping edge points
  doesn't change. And since the dropped segment was saturated (unusable)
  anyway, this isn't really a "loss" of good data -- it's the best
  achievable result from this file, not a compromise chosen for
  convenience.

## akhelabs acquisition software fix: no more sacrificing the first 10nm

Carolina changed the akhelabs software that drives the laser and the
CoreDAQ (not this repo's analysis code) to stop losing the first ~10nm of
every sweep to the detector-saturation transient documented throughout
today's log. Her own summary of the root cause and fix, verbatim:

> Bestätigt: coreDAQ-1 lief mit dem "Anti-saturation range"-Häkchen schon
> auf der niedrigsten Empfindlichkeit (5 mW Vollausschlag, Range-Index 0)
> -- trotzdem sättigt es. Das ist also kein Software-Range-Problem,
> sondern ein echter optischer Transient: der Laser braucht beim
> Sweep-Start eine feste Wellenlängen-Distanz zum Einschwingen (nicht
> Zeit), und ohne EDFA/eVOA im Pfad gibt es keine andere Stellschraube
> als den Sweep selbst.
>
> Ich habe in exfo_window.py ein neues Feld "Settle margin (nm)"
> hinzugefügt (nur bei coredaq-1/LINEAR sichtbar, Default 10 nm):
>
> Der physische Sweep startet jetzt margin nm vor deinem eingestellten
> Start (z.B. bei Start=1520, Margin=10 -> Laser fängt real bei 1510 nm
> an zu fahren), damit der Einschwing-Transient schon vorbei ist, bevor
> der Laser in deinen eigentlichen Messbereich kommt.
> Nach der Aufnahme werden genau diese Vorlauf-Samples wieder
> herausgeschnitten (exfo_window.py:918-927), sodass das Ergebnis exakt
> bei deinem ursprünglichen Start/Stop beginnt/endet -- nur eben ohne den
> Einschwingbereich in den Daten.
> Bei manueller Samples/Rate-Eingabe (statt Auto) musst du ggf.
> "Samples/ch" erhöhen, damit trotz des längeren physischen Sweeps noch
> genug Punkte im eigentlichen Zielbereich landen (Tooltip weist darauf
> hin).
> Bitte einmal mit 10 nm Margin testen und schauen, ob die ersten 10 nm
> jetzt sauber sind -- falls der Transient länger/kürzer als 10 nm ist,
> einfach den Wert anpassen.

**Practical consequence for this repo's pipeline:** every earlier
2026-08-20/2026-08-19 scan needed a manual wavelength-based trim
(`_trimmed1530.npz`, or `_trimmed1533.npz` for the longer-transient

- **Note (chat session, why the fiber peaks sat at ~1.7m/3.7m instead of
