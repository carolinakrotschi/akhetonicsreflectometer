  **Hypothesis tested: is this an aux-calibration artifact** (i.e. does
  forcing each scan's dL to its filename-indicated nominal aux length --
  4/5/6 m -- instead of the fringe-counting-calibrated tau_aux fix it)?
  Reprocessed all 13 scans in the comparison plot with `--dl` forced to
  nominal instead of `--tau-aux-ns` calibrated
  (`results/2026-08-31/aux_corrected/`,
  `results/2026-08-31/fiber_comparison_aux_corrected.png`). Two opposite
  effects found:
  - **Cross-scan connector-position consistency gets WORSE under the
    nominal-dL correction**: 587mm-family spread widens from ~1 mm
    (calibrated) to ~10 mm (aux-corrected); ~495mm-family spread widens
    from ~1.1 mm to ~3.5 mm. This argues the calibrated tau_aux is the
    physically correct one for internal consistency.
  - **But fiber-length accuracy vs. nominal gets MUCH BETTER** under the
    same nominal-dL correction: every fiber lands within ~2% of its
    round-number label (~1m fibers -> 1002-1019 mm; 3m fibers ->
    2913-2924 mm; 3m+1m series -> 3939 mm), versus 9-13% off under
    calibration.
  **Revised interpretation** (correcting an earlier, incomplete verbal
  conclusion in this same session that only looked at connector
  consistency): this pattern is more consistent with the fringe-counting
  calibration in `check_aux_interferometer.py` carrying a small,
  roughly-constant multiplicative bias (~10-15%, independent of the true
  aux length), which the nominal/intended aux length happens to
  approximately cancel because the aux was apparently built close to its
  intended target -- rather than the aux fiber itself being physically
  ~50 cm short. Not yet root-caused; **candidate next step: verify the
  aux fiber's true physical length independently (tape measure), same
  kind of check HANDOVER.md section 1 already calls for on the 46.4 mm
  topology question.**

  **Checked one specific mechanism and ruled it out:** does the
  calibration's "used fraction" (`m/n` in `check_aux_interferometer.py`,
  the fraction remaining after the `--trim` edge-crop) fail to account
  for the ~10 nm of sweep-start data historically dropped for detector
  saturation (the `_trimmed1530.npz` files)? Checked the actual
  historical calibration commands: `2026-08-19_1miodatapoints_trimmed1530.npz`
  and `deltaLwirklich1m_trimmed1530.npz` were both calibrated with
  `--lam-start 1530` (matching the true post-trim start, not the nominal
  1520), and their tau_aux values agree to within 0.03% of each other --
  which would not happen if one had a mismatched lam-start and the other
  didn't. So the historical scans were calibrated correctly with respect
  to this specific mechanism. And today's scans never needed a manual
  wavelength trim at all (settle-margin fix, raw `.json` files, full
  1520-1570 nm range matches `--lam-start`/`--lam-stop` exactly) yet show
  the same magnitude of shortfall -- ruling this mechanism out as the
  (sole) cause for today's data too. **Root cause of the ~10-15% bias
  remains open.**

  **Conceptual note, for the record:** clarified that tau_aux is the
  aux MZI's own arm-delay (n_g·dL/c, order ~17-26 ns here) -- NOT the
  sweep's acquisition duration (order ~0.83 s, ~8 orders of magnitude
  larger). The sweep is only the tool used to CALIBRATE tau_aux (by
  counting interference fringes over the sweep's known frequency span);
  tau_aux itself describes the interferometer's arm-length imbalance.
